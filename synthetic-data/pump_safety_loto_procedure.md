## 2. Purpose

This procedure defines the minimum safety controls required before inspection, maintenance,
disassembly, alignment, bearing work, seal replacement, or other intervention on pump
P-204A.

The procedure focuses on:

- isolation of electrical and process energy,
- lockout/tagout,
- verification of zero-energy condition,
- permit requirements,
- PPE,
- safe access and work-area control,
- controlled restart and post-maintenance handover.

---

## 3. Scope

This procedure applies to work involving:

- pump P-204A,
- connected suction and discharge lines,
- motor M-204A,
- local electrical isolation,
- associated valves and drains,
- coupling and rotating components,
- mechanical seal and bearing areas.

It applies to planned maintenance and authorized corrective maintenance.

It does **not** replace site emergency procedures, confined-space procedures, electrical
work procedures, hot-work procedures, or other specialist permits when those controls are
also applicable.

## 4. Known Hazard Categories

Before starting work, the work team shall consider the following hazards.

### 4.1 Electrical energy

Potential hazard from the motor starter, local isolator, MCC supply, control circuits,
and stored electrical energy.

### 4.2 Process pressure

Residual pressure may remain in the pump casing or connected pipework even after the
pump is stopped.

### 4.3 Stored fluid / chemical energy

The system may contain pressurized process or cooling water. Drainage and venting shall
be completed before opening equipment.

### 4.4 Rotating equipment

Coupling, shaft, fan, or other rotating parts may move unexpectedly if the equipment is
not isolated.

### 4.5 Unexpected startup

Remote or automatic control logic may attempt to start the pump unless the equipment is
properly isolated and the operating mode is controlled.

### 4.6 Slips and housekeeping

Water leakage, drained fluid, lubricants, tools, and temporary hoses may create slip or
trip hazards.

### 4.7 Manual handling

Pump components, covers, coupling parts, and tools may require mechanical lifting or
team handling.

## 7. Lockout / Tagout (LOTO) Sequence

### Step 1 — Identify the equipment

Confirm:

- Equipment tag: **P-204A**
- Motor tag: **M-204A**
- Work order / job reference
- Exact maintenance scope

Confirm the correct equipment before isolation.

### Step 2 — Notify affected personnel

Inform the relevant control-room operator, field operator, maintenance team, and other
affected personnel that P-204A will be removed from service.

### Step 3 — Stop the pump

Stop P-204A using the approved operating control.

Confirm that normal operating status indicates the pump has stopped.

### Step 4 — Isolate electrical supply

Open the designated electrical isolation for motor M-204A.

Apply the approved personal/group lock and tag arrangement according to site LOTO rules.

### Step 5 — Isolate process connections

Close the designated suction and discharge isolation valves.

Where the maintenance scope requires additional isolation, isolate all relevant connected
energy sources identified by the approved isolation plan.

### Step 6 — Depressurize and drain

Open the approved drain / vent points as applicable.

Confirm that trapped pressure and contained fluid have been safely relieved before opening
the pump casing or connected equipment.

### Step 7 — Prevent unexpected movement

Where required for the maintenance task:

- secure rotating components,
- prevent shaft movement,
- maintain coupling protection during work,
- use appropriate mechanical restraints.

### Step 8 — Verify zero-energy state

This is a mandatory verification step.

The authorized person shall verify that:

- the pump cannot start from the control system,
- electrical isolation is confirmed using the approved method,
- process pressure has been relieved,
- relevant lines have been drained,
- no hazardous stored energy remains.

**Do not rely only on the position of a switch or valve.**

### Step 9 — Apply locks / tags

Every person working under the applicable LOTO system shall follow the approved personal
lock / tag requirements.

Locks and tags must remain under controlled ownership until the individual has completed
the required work and is cleared according to site procedure.

## 9. Safety Controls During Maintenance

### 9.1 Pump opening / line breaking

Before opening the casing or connected process equipment:

1. Reconfirm isolation.
2. Confirm no pressure indication remains.
3. Drain residual fluid through the approved route.
4. Use the required PPE for the contained fluid.
5. Position personnel to avoid direct exposure to potential release paths.

### 9.2 Coupling / rotating equipment

Before working near the coupling:

- verify electrical isolation,
- prevent unexpected motor rotation,
- keep hands clear of pinch points,
- use suitable tools,
- reinstall guards before return to service.

### 9.3 Bearing work

During bearing inspection or replacement:

- prevent contamination of bearings,
- keep lubricants and tools clean,
- avoid loose clothing near rotating equipment,
- use correct handling tools.

### 9.4 Mechanical seal work

During seal inspection/replacement:

- confirm the casing is drained,
- avoid direct contact with residual fluid,
- protect machined sealing surfaces,
- follow the approved installation method,
- do not operate the pump without required seal and guard components installed.

## 10. Abnormal Condition / Stop-Work Triggers

Stop work and notify the responsible supervisor if any of the following occurs:

- Unexpected pressure or fluid release
- Pump cannot be positively isolated
- Electrical isolation cannot be verified
- Unexpected movement or rotation
- Incorrect equipment identification
- Isolation boundary differs from the approved plan
- Unknown substance or condition is encountered
- Damaged lifting equipment or unsuitable tool is identified
- Required permit or control is missing
- Personnel are exposed to an uncontrolled hazard

Work may resume only after the hazard is controlled and the applicable authorization is
re-established.

---

## 11. Controlled Reinstatement and Restart

After maintenance is completed:

### 11.1 Mechanical completion

Confirm that:

- required components are installed,
- coupling/alignment work is complete,
- fasteners are secured,
- guards are fitted,
- tools and loose materials are removed,
- drains/vents are restored as required,
- the work area is clean.

### 11.2 LOTO clearance

The responsible supervisor shall confirm that all personnel are clear and that locks/tags
are removed according to the approved site procedure.

No person may remove another worker's personal lock unless the site's formal emergency /
lock-removal procedure is followed.

### 11.3 Valve / process restoration

Restore the approved valve configuration according to the operating procedure and
isolation plan.

### 11.4 Start-up

Start P-204A using the approved operating procedure.

Monitor the equipment for:

- abnormal vibration,
- abnormal noise,
- seal leakage,
- motor current / operating indication where applicable,
- discharge pressure,
- flow / process response,
- temperature trends where instrumented.

### 11.5 Post-start verification

Any abnormal condition shall be recorded and escalated before the equipment is accepted
for normal service.
