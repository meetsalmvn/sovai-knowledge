## 4. Maintenance Trigger Conditions

Initiate this procedure when one or more of the following are observed:

1. Vibration exceeds the site-defined operating alert level.
2. Repeated vibration is reported after previous alignment or bearing work.
3. Visible mechanical seal leakage exceeds the accepted operating condition.
4. Abnormal bearing temperature is reported.
5. Coupling condition requires inspection.
6. Preventive maintenance interval has been reached.
7. Operations or maintenance personnel identify abnormal pump behavior.

For the current demonstration scenario, the primary trigger is **recurring abnormal vibration with a history of coupling/alignment and mechanical seal work**.

## 6. Isolation and Safety Requirements

### 6.1 Mandatory isolation

Before opening guards, removing components, or starting mechanical work:

1. Stop the pump through the approved operating procedure.
2. Isolate the electrical supply to motor M-204A.
3. Apply Lockout/Tagout (LOTO) according to the applicable site procedure.
4. Isolate the process-side suction and discharge boundaries as required by the approved job plan.
5. Relieve pressure before opening any process connection.
6. Drain and depressurize the equipment where required.
7. Verify zero-energy / safe state before hands-on work.
8. Attempt a controlled start from the authorized operating interface to verify that the pump cannot start, without bypassing safety controls.

## 7. Inspection and Maintenance Sequence

### Step 1 — External condition inspection

Record:

- Pump casing condition
- Foundation/baseplate condition
- Anchor bolt condition
- Guard condition
- Oil/grease leakage
- Mechanical seal leakage
- Coupling guard condition
- Visible signs of rubbing, looseness or corrosion

Photograph significant abnormal conditions where permitted by site practice.

### Step 2 — Baseline vibration observation

Before dismantling, record the latest available vibration readings and compare them with previous history.

Document:

- Measurement point
- Date/time
- Reading
- Direction/axis where available
- Comparison with previous readings
- Any associated noise or temperature observation

Do not create or invent vibration values when historical measurements are unavailable.

### Step 3 — Coupling inspection

Inspect coupling condition for:

- Visible damage
- Wear
- Loose or missing fasteners
- Abnormal element condition
- Evidence of misalignment
- Guard interference

Record all findings before adjustment.

### Step 4 — Alignment verification

Perform alignment verification using the approved method and calibrated instrument.

Record:

- Initial alignment condition
- Horizontal/vertical readings as applicable
- Correction performed
- Final alignment readings
- Technician and instrument identification where required

Adjustment values must be based on actual measurements; do not infer values from historical records.

### Step 5 — Bearing inspection

Inspect bearing condition according to the approved maintenance scope.

Check for:

- Abnormal noise
- Excessive play
- Evidence of overheating
- Lubrication condition
- Visible contamination or damage

Replace the bearing only when the approved job scope and inspection findings require it.

### Step 6 — Mechanical seal inspection

Inspect for:

- Leakage
- Seal-face condition where accessible
- Gland/packing condition where applicable
- Associated shaft/sleeve condition
- Signs of overheating or scoring

If replacement is required, install the approved spare and record part details according to the maintenance work order.

### Step 7 — Baseplate and fastener inspection

Check:

- Foundation condition
- Anchor bolts
- Baseplate fasteners
- Visible looseness
- Signs of movement or soft-foot condition where applicable

Correct deficiencies within the approved scope.

### Step 8 — Lubrication

Apply the lubricant specified by the approved equipment maintenance data. Record lubricant type, quantity and date where required.

Do not substitute a lubricant based only on generic recommendations.

## 8. Post-Maintenance Checks

After maintenance is complete:

1. Confirm all tools, temporary materials and loose parts are removed.
2. Reinstall guards and protective covers.
3. Confirm all fasteners are secure.
4. Confirm process and mechanical connections are restored.
5. Close out the maintenance work scope according to the permit/LOTO process.
6. Obtain required authorization before re-energization and startup.
7. Start the pump under controlled operating conditions.
8. Check for abnormal noise, leakage, temperature and vibration.
9. Record post-maintenance vibration readings.
10. Compare post-maintenance observations with the available pre-maintenance baseline.
11. Escalate unresolved abnormalities before placing the pump into normal service.

---

## 9. Acceptance Criteria

The pump may be considered ready for service when:

- The approved maintenance scope is complete.
- Safety guards are restored.
- No unacceptable leakage is observed.
- No abnormal noise or mechanical interference is observed.
- Post-maintenance vibration observations are acceptable against the applicable site/equipment criteria.
- Required test results are recorded.
- Required work-order documentation is complete.
- Any outstanding issues are clearly recorded and assigned for follow-up.

**Note:** This synthetic SOP intentionally does not specify a universal numerical vibration acceptance limit. Use the applicable equipment/site standard for an actual job.

## 12. Escalation Conditions

Stop work and escalate when:

- Isolation cannot be positively verified.
- Unexpected stored energy is identified.
- Equipment condition differs materially from the approved scope.
- Structural, electrical or process hazards are identified.
- Severe damage is found that requires engineering assessment.
- Post-maintenance vibration remains abnormal or worsens.
- Leakage or operating conditions are outside the approved acceptance criteria.
